01. Quantum Tunneling / Wavepacket Simulator

★★ Easy 5–8 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

Throw a tennis ball at a brick wall: it bounces back every time. In the quantum world, if the wall is thin enough, the ball's probability wave leaks through — appearing on the other side without breaking the wall! This powers the Sun's nuclear fusion and scanning tunneling microscopes (STMs).

Live Wavepacket Split Animation
Incident Wave Energy Barrier Tunneled Wave (~30%)
🔬 Quantum Technique: Split-Operator Method
  1. Discretize 1D space into 2n grid points using n qubits.
  2. Apply potential energy V(x) as phase rotations in position space.
  3. Apply QFT (Quantum Fourier Transform) → switch to momentum space.
  4. Apply kinetic energy p2/2m phases → inverse QFT back to position.
💡 Portfolio Highlight

The QFT is used as an intuitive physical tool (converting position to momentum like an optical prism), rather than just an abstract algorithm.

⚠️ Hardware Limit: QFT circuits are deep and accumulate noise rapidly. Run 1–2 time steps on real IBM chips.

02. Neutrino Oscillation Simulator

★★ Basic ★★★★ Collective 1–16 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

Imagine a chameleon that changes color in a steady rhythm as it walks: green → blue → red → green. Neutrinos do this with their quantum flavors (νe, νμ, ντ). This won the 2015 Nobel Prize because it proved neutrinos have mass.

Flavor Probability vs. Distance Traveled
νe (Electron) νμ (Muon) ντ (Tau)
🔬 How It Works
  • 2 Flavors: 1 qubit (|0⟩ = νe, |1⟩ = νμ). Evolution is a single-qubit Ry(θ) rotation.
  • 3 Flavors: 2 qubits (using 3 of the 4 computational states).
  • Collective (Supernova): 4–16 qubits with all-to-all neutrino entanglement — active research territory!
💡 Portfolio Advantage

The basic version runs with exceptional fidelity on real IBM hardware because circuits require minimal gate depth. You can expand it to the MSW effect (neutrinos passing through Earth/Sun matter).

03. Quantum Magnetism / Spin Chain Dynamics

★★★ Medium 6–16 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

A row of compass needles where each needle pulls on its neighbors. Flip the middle needle: a wave of magnetic disturbance spreads left and right like ripples in water. This is the "Hello World" of quantum many-body physics.

Excitation Spreading ("Lieb-Robinson Light Cone")
⬆️ Ground state (|0⟩) ⬇️ Excited state (|1⟩) Time flows down ↓
🔬 Quantum Technique

Simulate 1D transverse-field Ising or Heisenberg models: H = -J ∑ σiz σi+1z - h ∑ σix Trotterize into layers of 2-qubit ZZ(θ) interactions and single-qubit RX(θ) rotations.

💡 Core Engine Backbone

This module maps 1:1 onto IBM's heavy-hex qubit topologies. Mastering this unlocks Project #7 (Time Crystals), #11 (Chaos), and #12 (Trotter study).

★★★★ Hard 4–12 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

"Empty" space isn't empty. When an intense electric field stresses the vacuum, matter and antimatter pairs (e- and e+) are ripped out of nothingness! You simulate this landmark quantum electrodynamics process.

🔬 Key Technique: Jordan-Wigner Transformation

Fermions (matter particles) anticommute. Jordan-Wigner maps fermionic creation/annihilation operators (ci†, ci) to Pauli spin strings on qubits.

🏆 Flagship Value

Reproduces the famous 2016 Nature paper (Martinez et al., Innsbruck). "Simulating matter created from the vacuum on a quantum chip" is an undeniable resume hook.

06. Nuclear Physics on the Cloud: Deuteron Binding Energy

★★ Easy 2–3 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

The deuteron is the simplest atomic nucleus: 1 proton + 1 neutron. How strong is the nuclear glue binding them together? (~2.22 MeV). Using VQE, a 2-qubit circuit calculates this fundamental constant on real quantum hardware with near-perfect accuracy.

07. Discrete Time Crystal

★★★ Medium 8–20 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

A diamond repeats in space. A time crystal repeats in time. Push a playground swing once every second, but the swing moves at twice the period (once every 2 seconds) and stubbornly resists timing imperfections!

Subharmonic Period-Doubling (2T) Signature
Drive Pulse (Period T) Spin Response (Period 2T)
🔬 How It Works

Apply periodic Floquet drives: imperfect π-pulses (Rx) + disordered Ising interactions (ZZ). Many-body localization prevents heating and locks the subharmonic response.

💡 Google Quantum Landmark

Demonstrated on Google's Sycamore chip in 2021. Reuses your spin-chain engine directly as Module #2.

08. Noise as a Feature: Photosynthetic Energy Transfer

★★★★ Hard 3–8 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

In a maze, zero randomness gets you trapped in dead ends (destructive interference). Too much randomness leaves you stumbling in circles (decoherence). A moderate amount of noise helps solar energy reach the reaction center with nearly 100% efficiency.

Environment-Assisted Quantum Transport (ENAQT)
🔬 Paradigm Shift

Instead of fighting quantum hardware noise, you utilize the chip's natural dephasing noise as the biological environment bath to demonstrate the transport sweet spot.

💡 Interview Talking Point

Bridges quantum open systems theory, quantum biology (FMO complex), and real quantum hardware imperfections.

10. Anyons & Topological Order: The Toric Code

★★★★ Hard 8–20 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

In 2D, exotic quasi-particles called anyons exist. When you wind one anyon around another, the wave function remembers the topological braid path! This is the bedrock of fault-tolerant quantum error correction (Surface Codes).

Toric Code Lattice & Anyon Braiding
🔬 What You Build

Prepare stabilizer ground states (Av = ∏ X, Bp = ∏ Z), create electric/magnetic anyon pairs, braid them, and detect topological phase shift.

💼 Quantum Industry Relevance

This is the foundational math behind error-corrected logical qubits used by Google, Quantinuum, and IBM.

12. The Trotter vs. Noise Trade-Off Study

★★★ Medium 4–10 Qubits
☕ Coffee Analogy

More simulation steps make the algorithm mathematically accurate, but add more physical gates which collect hardware noise! There is an optimal "sweet spot" step count. This is a scientific research experiment, not just a demo.

Total Simulation Error (The U-Shaped Curve)
🔬 Experimental Method
  1. Run on ideal simulator → measures algorithmic Trotter error O(Δt2).
  2. Run on noisy simulator → adds gate infidelity.
  3. Run on IBM Quantum backend → finds real-world minimum error point.
🎓 Research Credibility

Shows recruiters you understand the real physical trade-offs in modern NISQ-era quantum computing.
